## The Original Bathroom Problem

The pictures above are what the bathroom looked like previously (scroll down to the bottom of the post to see the finished product). The floor was some type of faux linoleum attached to a thin wood backing and as you can tell it was stained and oddly colored. The vanity was your standard builder grade cabinet with a particle board counter top. The sinks were the cheapest delta model you could find and were top mount so the rim never quite got cleaned. The drains were somewhat rusty the sink on the left (Mer's sink) would leak if you used hot water. The tub was the biggest hazard though, as it had a massive hole on the inside that the previous owners just put a big blob of epoxy over it to seal it. It looked terrible and it was one good poke away from causing a leak. The tub was also stained permanently and Mer said that due to this it never felt satisfying to clean, and thus seldomly was.

## Finding a Contractor

We wanted to do tile floors and a tile shower, so we knew we were not going to DIY this. Plus with a baby at the time, it would've taken forever to do ourselves since only one of us could work on it at any given time. Therefore, we needed to hire it out. We met with a few contractors beforehand but nobody really stood out until we met with a local general contractor (GC) with the company <a href="https://www.benjamindesignbuild.com/">Benjamin Design Build</a>. They were very welcoming to our project and could get it done quickly for us and at a really good price too. They showed us previous projects they had done, gave us a quote on the spot, and let us customize it however we wanted. We got this bathroom done in early 2026 and at the end it ended up being about ~15k for everything (labor, materials, etc.) and they got it done in under a month. We have since worked with them 2 other times and have had great experiences each time. If you are local to the area, I would highly recommend contacting them for any remodeling or renovation needs.

## DIY parts

We did not let the contractor do everything to save a bit on costs. The stuff we did ourselves included: Painting (walls and trim), re-installing the existing toilet, installing the towel hooks, and installing the mirrors. I also swapped out all the old receptacles, light switches, and the vent register to new, fresh white ones. All of this stuff was pretty simple, but it ended up saving us about $500 or so doing it ourselves.

## Finished Product

Below is what the finished product looks like. We are extremely happy with how it turned out and would not change a thing about it. Overall, this was a pretty easy experience and something that adds value to our home while also being something we can really enjoy and take advantage of. This was definitely our biggest project we've ever done on the house and it was worth it in the end. Not only is it gorgeous, but its functional and will last a long time.
